## DECIDED 2026-09-17 by Henry: the dialog note stays, as fixture-verified only

**The fixture check answers "can it fire as built": yes.** `mcp-live.spec`'s *"a live audit of a page locked
behind a dialog says the walk scrolled the dialog"* passes on `dialog-locked.html`. The readiness record says
it fired identically on all five fixture runs.

**Why it has never fired live, read from the code rather than guessed.** `walkDialogNote` fires only on one
shape: the page hides the document's overflow **and** the only scroller the walk finds sits inside a
`<dialog>`, `[role=dialog]`, `[role=alertdialog]` or `[aria-modal=true]` **in the page's own DOM**
(`scrollHost.ts`'s `inDialog`). The consent walls met live take other routes, and each is named by a true
sentence:
- **A wall inside an iframe:** ft.com, run 15. One frame covered 100% of the viewport, and
  `walkNothingNote` named it as a wall. A dialog in a frame isn't in the page's DOM, so the dialog note
  can't claim it, and shouldn't.
- **An in-page dialog with no scroller of its own:** a first-layer banner shorter than the screen. The walk
  finds nothing to scroll, and `walkNothingNote` says so.

**So the note isn't broken, and isn't dead code:** its shape is narrower than "a dialog-walled site". It's
a scroll-locked page whose dialog is itself taller than its box. **Keep it, fixture-verified only.** Retire
it if a live page of exactly that shape is measured and the note stays silent. That would be a defect, not
a rarity.

## DECIDED 2026-09-17 by Henry: enter open shadow roots, by default, with no flag

**The evidence the decision was waiting on exists now:**
- **caniuse.com** (run 17, 2026-09-15): *"57 shadow roots hold 188 of this page's 238 text elements, which
  the measurement does not enter"*, so 79% of the page is unmeasured, though declared.
- **chromestatus.com** (run 0.56.0): 159 shadow hosts, and the page's 136 interactive and 147 text-bearing
  elements are all inside them. Before 0.57 it measured as empty.
- **youtube.com:** zero open hosts, and a correct silence. The share note fires on its condition, not on
  the framework.
- The gap the 0.58.0 candidate found (shares reported only for pages that measured empty) is closed: the
  note fires at 15% or 25 elements (`SHADOW_SHARE_FLOOR`), which is how caniuse was caught.

**Why enter, not keep naming the share:** Obsrv's job is to measure what a reader sees. On a page built from
Lit, Polymer, Stencil, FAST or Ionic components, an audit that declares 79% unmeasured is an honest
non-answer. Open roots are reachable from script, so there's no technical reason to stop at the boundary.
The note was the right stopgap, not the right product.

**Why no flag:** the correct measurement should be the default. An agent won't know to pass a flag, and two
behaviours double what has to be tested and explained.

**Delivered by `feat-measure-open-shadow-roots`** (filed with this decision, with scope and acceptance).
Until it ships, the share note stays exactly as it is, and `docs/limitations.md` says the decision is made
and not yet implemented. Closed roots stay unreachable by design. This is an engineering decision, and
Opeyemi can overrule it.
