# 0.49.0 driven against five more live sites

The eighth run, on a stack that is 0.49.0 end to end — the app relaunched
from the new DMG, the plugin updated, the session's MCP server started after
the publish — against en.wikipedia.org (dense text and tables, as the
control), wired.com (a magazine with `srcset` cards and ads), asos.com (a
lazy retail grid rendered on the client), stackoverflow.com (1 px borders, a
cookie card, and, it turned out, a bot challenge) and docs.github.com (a
dark documentation shell). booking.com and lemonde.fr got a regression probe
each first, since 0.49.0 exists because of them. Headless, live, and every
surface. The site is the fixture; this reports on the tool.

## Held

- **The three 0.49.0 fixes, on the pages that raised them.** booking.com
  at `iphone-61` with no wait: 70 targets and 85 text elements, no
  "nothing to measure", and the coverage note saying honestly that the page
  grew behind the walk. lemonde.fr's "Reject all cookies": set aside as text
  on an image instead of failing at 1.14:1 on a white nobody sees. Every
  lint answer carried `skipped.spacers`, 0 on all five sites.
- **The image rules by fit.** wired.com at a phone: "768×768 px (the 768w
  candidate) covering 160×160 device px (object-fit: cover): downsampled
  4.8×"; asos: six lazy-loaded cards upscaled 1.08×.
- **The live review loop** down the Wikipedia article: five screenfuls seen
  and a `bottom` at 8,473 px, every capture settled, then a live audit that
  walked 12 screenfuls and measured 827 text elements with none under the
  threshold. docs.github.com on Budget TN, live: `#848d97` on `#21262d`
  passes as stated at 4.52:1 and fails on the panel at 2.9:1, the same seven
  pairs the headless lint found. `report` wrote both Docs screens; `diff`
  on Wikipedia settled and named three bands.
- **The honest refusals.** asos.com refused a load that ran out, in one
  line, then loaded in four seconds a minute later (its ad network's
  doing). A headless snap of Stack Overflow's mobile page said "the frame is
  one colour end to end (#ffffff) and stayed that way for 3000 ms": true,
  and the only hint of what follows. `inspect` on an ad button that had not
  loaded answered `found: false`, as documented.
- **Two things that looked wrong and were not.** Wikipedia set aside 57 text
  elements as sitting on images: its external links carry an icon as a
  background image, and the old rule said the same. A Docs hero paragraph
  came back "image" on a dark card: the browser's own stack shows a
  translucent layer over a container whose background is the hero artwork
  as an `image-set`, and that container is the paragraph's ancestor, so the
  old walk answered the same. The page composes it that way.

## Broke or misled, ranked

1. **Nothing budgets a measurement after `load`, and a bot challenge holds
   it for minutes.** `obsrv_audit` on stackoverflow.com/questions at
   `android-65` "did not exit within 90000 ms and was terminated". Through
   the CLI the same audit finished, with a full and correct answer, after
   142 s, 135 s without the walk, and 204 s in a timed rerun; a rerun with
   phase timestamps shows why. Stack Overflow fronts a Cloudflare challenge
   for a new client — a browser with a fresh mobile profile shows "Just a
   moment..." before the page — and the CLI's profile is new every run. The
   challenge document loads in 1.1 s with nothing in it, so the
   empty-document grace re-asks the page five times over three seconds, and
   the sixth ask blocks inside `executeJavaScriptInIsolatedWorld` for as
   long as the challenge takes: 9.4 s on a good run, three minutes and
   more on a bad one, every renderer process at 0 % CPU the whole time.
   The load budget (`timeoutMs`) has nothing to say about it, because the
   load finished; the only bound is the MCP's kill at renders × (timeout +
   wait) + 60 s, which throws the answer away. The live app measured the
   same tab in 2.5 s, because its persistent profile passed the challenge
   once. A measurement needs a budget of its own after the load — a race
   against a timer around the page ask, answering with what it has and a
   sentence naming the wait — and a page that navigates itself after `load`
   (a challenge, an interstitial, a redirect) should be named in the
   answer, since the figures are of the page it arrived at.

2. **A killed CLI's error is 2 KB of raw chatter.** The 0.48.0 filter
   strips Chromium's stderr and falls back to the raw tail "when chatter is
   all there is", on the theory that something is better than nothing.
   When the CLI is killed it has never written a line of its own, so the
   raw tail is what comes back: two kilobytes of tracker URLs with
   `ERR_BLOCKED_BY_RESPONSE`, `ERR_NAME_NOT_RESOLVED` and SSL handshake
   codes, and no sentence about the tool. A killed CLI should say that it
   was killed, in which phase if it knows, and that Chromium logged N lines
   of its own — not print them.

## Fixed on `fix/measure-budget-and-kill-text`

1. **A measurement has a budget of its own after the load.** `--timeout`
   (`timeoutMs`) now bounds the load and then, separately, everything after
   it: the walk, the wait for a document with nothing in it, and the page
   ask, each raced against the remaining budget (`shared/measureBudget`).
   A page ask that the budget wins answers with nothing and a first warning
   that says why, what the figures are, and what to do; a page that
   navigates itself after `load` is given the rest of the budget to arrive,
   then walked and measured where it arrived, and the answer names the
   move. The app's control audit and lint bound their asks the same way,
   so live answers say it too. stackoverflow.com through the CLI, twice
   with the default budget: 33 s to zeros with "did not answer the audit
   within 30 s of loading: its main thread was busy or blocked after
   navigating to the same address — a bot challenge …", and once, when
   the challenge cleared in time, 14 s to 154 targets with "the page
   navigated after it loaded (to the same address) … the figures are of
   the page it arrived at". A fixture that holds its main thread for forty
   seconds after load pins the CLI, the lint, the inspector and the MCP
   within a 3 s budget; a fixture that moves on to another page 400 ms
   after load pins the arrival.

2. **A killed CLI says what is known.** The message names the kill, the
   CLI's own last line if it wrote one or that it wrote nothing, and how
   many lines Chromium logged with the last one clipped, and it says what
   the budget bounds; the raw tail is gone from that path.

Observed and left alone: the empty-document grace overshoots when a single
ask blocks (`waitedMs` 10,677 against a 3,000 ms grace), which is the same
finding from the other side; Wikipedia's `diff` gained 4.5 pp of ink in the
band holding a thumbnail, where the 2x reference loads a denser `srcset`
candidate, a known limit of comparing rasters that carry images.
