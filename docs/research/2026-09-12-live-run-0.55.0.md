# 0.55.0 driven against a fresh set of live sites

The twelfth run, and the first on a stack that is 0.55.0 end to end — the
app relaunched from the new DMG, the plugin updated and the session
restarted, the MCP server fetched from the registry — against cnn.com,
nytimes.com, theguardian.com, spiegel.de, news.ycombinator.com,
pinterest.com and quora.com. Headless at a desktop preset unless said
otherwise. The site is the fixture; this reports on the tool.

The stack was checked before it was trusted, and behaviourally: the
session's own `obsrv` answers the dialog fixture with 0.55.0's sentence,
its plugin pins `getobsrv@0.55.0`, `/Applications/Obsrv.app` reports
0.55.0 and the registry's `latest` is 0.55.0.

## Held

- **Nothing was dropped, anywhere.** Not one page in this run lost an
  entry to the checks — no `dropped` count on any site, at any preset.
  That is the reassurance 0.55.0's second fix needed: the new tolerance
  is not quietly absorbing a producer and a validator that disagree. Had
  the srcset fault of 0.54.0 still been live, this run would have said so
  in numbers.
- **The rules find things worth finding.** news.ycombinator.com at
  android-65: 32 of 32 targets under 7 mm, thirty of them the 1.88 mm
  upvote arrows. nytimes.com: 103 of 148 targets under, including an 8 px
  wide button at 2.21 mm. spiegel.de: 526 targets, 165 under, on a 34,582
  px page. cnn.com: 166 targets, 37 under.
- **A load that never finishes is still measured.** cnn.com and
  nytimes.com both outran the 30 s budget and were measured as they
  stood, each saying so first — 0.53.0's fix, holding on real pages a
  release later.
- **A walk that could not run says it did not run.** cnn.com's main
  thread never answered a scroll: "the walk was cut short before it began
  … measured without walking", rather than a silent zero.
- **The dialog note stayed quiet, correctly, everywhere.** Every site
  walked several screenfuls of the root, and a page held by a modal
  cannot do that. No false positive in seven sites.

## Broke or misled, ranked

1. **The walk-coverage note leads with a cause the tool has already ruled
   out.** theguardian.com walked 9 screenfuls of a 21,440 px page,
   spiegel.de 10 of 34,582 px, nytimes.com 6 of 9,741 px — and each was
   told:

   > the walk saw the end after 9 screenfuls (10800 CSS px), but the page
   > measures 21440 CSS px (20 screenfuls): **a modal or a locked scroll
   > held the page**, or it grew after the walk

   A page whose root scrolled nine screenfuls and reported `atEnd` was
   manifestly not held by a modal. The tool knows this at the moment it
   writes the sentence: it knows the walk moved the root, how far, and
   that it reached the end. Leading with the modal sends the reader
   hunting for something that cannot be there — and on all three of these
   pages the real cause is the clause that comes last, a feed that grows
   as it is walked.

   The fix is to say which: when the walk moved the root and reached its
   end, the page grew, and the note should say so plainly; the modal
   clause belongs to the case where the walk went nowhere. Three
   independent instances in one run, all on ordinary news front pages.

## Not validated by this run

**The dialog note has still never fired on a live site.** Seven were
tried, including the consent-wall publishers the fix was written for;
every one served a clean, scrollable page to a fresh headless profile —
theguardian's snap shows no consent layer at all, and pinterest and quora
gave their sign-in pages rather than a modal over content. So 0.55.0's
first fix remains pinned on a fixture and unproven in the field. That is
a gap in the evidence, not a defect: the mechanism is real and
reproducible, and consent walls are region-gated in ways this machine
does not trigger. Worth trying again from a network that draws them, or
against a product's own staging where the modal can be opened on demand.
