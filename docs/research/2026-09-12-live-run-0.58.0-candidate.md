# Run 15: two notes that guess while holding the answer

The fifteenth run, against ikea.com (a retailer, and a web-components
guess), gov.uk (a site built to be accessible — the interesting result is
what it does *not* draw), ft.com (a paywall behind a consent wall), and
linear.app (a dark SPA). Driven headless from main@72db2eb.

Two of the four were chosen for open finish-line items rather than for
coverage: the dialog note has never fired on a live site, and the
shadow-traversal decision is waiting on evidence about how often components
hide a page. Both items moved, and neither moved the way the last three
runs assumed they would.

Ground truth for each page's shape was taken in a second browser,
independently of Obsrv, so "what the wall actually is" is not the tool's own
answer to its own question. That browser carries different consent state
than Obsrv's profile, which matters once below.

## Held

- **gov.uk draws almost nothing, and that is the right answer.** 10
  targets, 1 under 7 mm, 1 finding, no warnings. The check that this was
  not under-measurement: of 125 elements matching the audit's own target
  selector, **102 are inline links with text**, which WCAG 2.5.8 exempts
  and the audit skips by design. A site of prose and links should produce
  almost no target findings, and it does. A rule that fired here would be
  more likely wrong than gov.uk would.
- **linear.app: 133 targets, 25 under 7 mm**, and the contrast rules
  correctly declined 117 text elements sitting on images or gradients
  rather than guessing a verdict.
- **The locked-page walk note fired on a real consent wall.** ft.com: "the
  walk saw the end after 0 screenfuls (1080 CSS px), but the page measures
  8337 CSS px (8 screenfuls)". The measurement did not pretend the page was
  one screen tall.

## Broke or misled, ranked

1. **A page can hide most of itself behind shadow roots and Obsrv says
   nothing — while holding the count.** `shadowContent()` runs on *every*
   measurement (`src/shared/lint.ts:469`, unconditional) and the counts
   reach the report. But `report.shadow` is read in exactly four places and
   every one is inside a `stillEmpty` guard — `src/cli/main.ts:995` and
   `:1112` headless, `src/main/ipc.ts:1645` and `:1689` live — so the
   numbers are reported only when the light DOM is **completely** empty, on
   every surface, in the app as much as in the CLI (checked independently
   by obsrv-4f).

   Measured on a fixture with 12 buttons in the light DOM and 40 more
   inside four open shadow roots: **12 targets, 12 findings, 0 warnings.**
   The answer reads as a complete measurement of a page it measured a
   quarter of.

   The empty case is the rare one and it is the only one covered. The
   partial case is the common one — a design system with a few
   custom-element widgets on an ordinary page — and there the figures are
   quietly incomplete with no signal at all. That is worse than the case
   0.57.0 fixed, because a reader of "nothing to measure" at least knows
   something is wrong.

   It also means the evidence 0.57.0 promised is not being collected. Its
   release notes said "every run from here reports how often components
   hide a page and how much they hide, which is the evidence for deciding
   whether to enter shadow roots at all". Every run since has reported that
   only for pages that measure as nothing, so the traversal decision has
   been waiting on evidence that the code does not emit.

   **The fix is a ratio, not a flag, and the ratio is the design question.**
   A page with one decorative component behind a root should stay silent; a
   page with 40 of 52 targets hidden should say so plainly. Where between
   those the sentence starts is a judgement that should be made by printing
   the output at several shapes before choosing, not by picking a number
   and testing that it works.

2. **The walk's "nothing to scroll" note offers three causes and measures
   two of them.** ft.com's wall locked the page, and the note said: content
   "in an iframe, in a shadow root, or in a container that scrolls by
   transform … was not brought into view".

   What ft.com actually is, measured independently: `body { position:
   fixed; overflow: hidden }`, **one iframe covering 100% of the viewport**
   (`consent-manager.ft.com`), inside a `div[role="dialog"]` of 1024×768,
   and **zero shadow hosts**. The first guess is right, the second is
   false, and Obsrv already measures both — `framesInViewport()` runs
   beside `shadowContent()` on the same unconditional line.

   `WALK_NOTHING_NOTE` (`src/shared/walkCoverage.ts:71`) is a **constant
   string**. It cannot name a cause because it takes no arguments. This is
   the same defect the empty-document note had before 0.57.0 — a list of
   guesses in front of a measured fact — surviving in the note next door.

3. **The dialog note's shape is not the shape live walls have.** Nine
   sites over three runs, now ten over four, and it has still never fired.
   ft.com is the first live wall caught in the act, and it shows why:
   `walkDialogNote` is for *a dialog that was scrolled instead of the
   page*, and ft.com's dialog **is not scrollable** (`scrollHeight <=
   clientHeight`) — it is a full-viewport iframe inside a fixed body. The
   walk found no scroller anywhere, so note 2 fired and the dialog note
   could not.

   So the item is no longer "the note has not been seen". It is: the wall
   that locks a page and the dialog that steals the walk are **two
   different shapes**, and only the second has a note. The first currently
   arrives as a generic three-guess sentence, ranked above.

## What this run says about readiness

A test for any silence the tool produces, which came out of arguing about
finding 1 with obsrv-4f and generalises past it:

> Name the two facts that would each produce this silence. If they are
> opposite facts, the silence is a defect rather than a quiet success.

Obsrv's silence on a page with no shadow roots and its silence on a page
hiding 40 of 52 targets are the same output for opposite facts, which is
why finding 1 is a defect and not a tool being appropriately quiet. Every
silence fixed in the last week has had that shape: the blank window that
was a lost WebGL context, the 404 measured as though it were the page, the
redirect during load, the empty light DOM of a components page. Each was
indistinguishable from the case where nothing was wrong.

The test is cheap enough to apply before shipping a note rather than after
a live run finds it, which is the only reason it is worth writing down.

## Still open, unchanged

A page behind a real login is still unmeasured — it needs credentials,
which is not something to put through a tool session. ft.com's paywall is
the nearest thing reached so far, and it gates content rather than the
page.

## A hypothesis that did not survive

ikea.com was chosen as a web-components candidate — a large retailer with a
public design system. It has **zero open shadow hosts** (276 interactive
elements, all light DOM). So does gov.uk, so does linear.app, and so does
ft.com. With youtube.com's zero in run 14, that is **five consecutive live
sites at zero**, against chromestatus.com's 159.

Recorded because it points the traversal decision somewhere specific: on
the evidence so far, shadow roots are rare on public marketing and content
sites and concentrated in tooling and dashboards.

One caveat on how much those five zeros are worth, because it is easy to
state too strongly in either direction. They were counted **directly, in a
second browser**, by walking every open root on the page — so they are real
measurements of those pages and would have caught a partial case had there
been one. What they are not is evidence that Obsrv would have told anyone:
its silence on those five is indistinguishable from its silence on the
fixture that hid 40 of 52 targets. The sites are genuinely at zero; the
tool's agreement with that is a coincidence of the two facts, not a
confirmation. Fix finding 1 and the coincidence stops mattering — the next
three runs will answer the question without a second browser.

## Where these landed

Findings 1 and 2 were fixed the same day, in `fix/name-what-is-hidden`
(merged as ac721af) and `fix/shadow-count-that-scales`. Recorded here
because the run and the fix are usually a release apart, and a reader
finding this report later should not have to guess whether the silences it
describes are still there. Finding 3 — that locking a page and stealing the
walk are two different shapes, and only the second has a note — is a
product decision and is still open.

The first fix immediately grew a defect of its own: the share floor was
calibrated on fifty-element pages, so 60 controls hidden on a 560-control
page stayed silent. That was found by printing shapes rather than by
another live run, which is the cheaper of the two loops and the one this
report argues for.
